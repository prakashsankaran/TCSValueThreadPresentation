AUTHORITATIVE PROBLEM STATEMENT BRIEF

Transforming Business Analysis

From Fragmented Ideas to Traceable Requirements and Measurable Value
1. Problem Statement

Organisations begin transformation initiatives, product enhancements and technology projects with a business idea, operational challenge, customer need or regulatory requirement. However, the information required to understand that need rarely exists in one complete and reliable source.

Business needs are typically scattered across:
Emails and chat conversations
Meeting discussions and transcripts
Business documents and presentations
Policies, standards and regulatory guidelines
Existing system behaviour
Customer complaints and operational incidents
Stakeholder assumptions and individual viewpoints
Previous requirements and project artefacts

Each source provides only a part of the overall context. As a result, business analysts and product owners must manually interpret fragmented information, reconcile conflicting viewpoints, identify missing decisions and convert informal discussions into requirements that delivery teams can understand and implement.

2. Why This Problem Matters

The main challenge is not simply documenting what stakeholders have said. The deeper challenge is determining:

What business problem actually needs to be solved
Why the problem is important
Who is affected by it
Which outcomes the organisation expects
What assumptions have been made
Which stakeholder statements conflict with one another
What information is missing
Which policies and constraints must be considered
How success will be accepted and measured
Ambiguity Example: "Immediate Approval"

Stakeholders may use the same words while intending different outcomes. For example: A business stakeholder requests an "immediate approval", operations interprets it as within the same day, security policy requires additional review for high-risk cases, while development implements full automation. Unless resolved early, the solution fails.

3. Current-State Challenge

In many organisations, business analysis follows a largely manual, document-centric process where fragmented inputs are manually interpreted into specifications.

During this process:
  • Important business context may be lost
  • Assumptions may be treated as confirmed decisions
  • Contradictions may remain undetected
  • Missing information may not be identified early
  • Requirements may be duplicated across documents
  • Policy constraints discovered only during design/testing
  • Acceptance criteria fail to represent business intent
4. Business Impact & Risks

Unresolved ambiguity creates severe downstream consequences throughout the delivery lifecycle:

  • Repeated clarification meetings & requirement churn
  • Incorrect solution design & development rework
  • Increased testing effort & missed security requirements
  • Defects caused by misunderstood expectations
  • Delayed stakeholder acceptance & increased project cost
  • Longer delivery timelines & reduced stakeholder confidence
  • Solutions built that provide limited business value
5. The Fundamental Problem

Traditional requirement practices often focus on recording stakeholder requests rather than challenging and validating the underlying business need.

Requested Feature:
"Build a dashboard."
Actual Business Need:
• Identify operational delays & reduce manual reporting
• Improve management visibility & detect compliance breaches
• Support faster, data-driven decisions

Therefore, the objective of modern business analysis should not be limited to transcription. It must support discovery of intent, challenge of assumptions, reconciliation of viewpoints, and measurement of value.

6. The Challenge to Be Solved (8 Journey Stages)

Design a reusable, framework-first methodology that transforms fragmented inputs into structured, validated, approved and traceable requirements supporting the complete journey:

1
Expressed: Capture initial ideas, requests, incidents, or policy changes from multiple sources.
2
Explored: Understand broader context, stakeholders, process, objectives, constraints, and policies.
3
Challenged: Question initial requests, surface ambiguous terminology, conflicts, and missing decisions.
4
Structured: Convert validated understanding into reusable requirement artefacts (Stories, NFRs, Rules).
5
Approved: Ensure business analysts and product owners formally review and approve intended meaning.
6
Built & Tested: Maintain unbroken traceability to designs, architecture, code, test scenarios, and releases.
7
Accepted: Demonstrate that delivered solution satisfies approved requirements based on concrete evidence.
8
Measured for Value: Evaluate whether delivered solution achieved expected business outcomes (ROI, speed, cost).
7. Role of Large Language Models

LLMs process large volumes of unstructured info to summarize chats, extract needs, detect ambiguity, compare viewpoints, generate clarification questions, and assess requirement completeness.

Boundary: AI must NOT act as an autonomous decision-maker. AI surfaces uncertainty; it does not decide meaning.
8. Human Accountability (HitL)

Business Analysts & Product Owners remain strictly accountable for resolving ambiguity and approving meaning:

  • AI: Discovers, structures info & proposes questions
  • Analysts: Investigate context & clarify expectations
  • Product Owners: Make prioritisation decisions
  • Authorised Humans: Approve final meaning
9. Characteristics of the Methodology
Reusable: Works across industries & tech platforms
Framework-First: Repeatable methodology
Source-Agnostic: Accepts chats, docs, policies & systems
Challenge-Oriented: Questions needs vs transcribing
Traceable: Linked from source to value outcome
Governed: Role-based human approvals & audit
Change-Aware: Identifies downstream impact on changes
Explainable: Traces why & who approved every decision
Secure: Enterprise data protection & access controls
Value-Driven: Continues until outcome is measured
10. Scope of Reimagination & Definition of Success

The goal is to move from documents to connected evidence, static requirements to living traceability, and feature delivery to measurable business value.

A successful solution demonstrates:
  1. Fragmented inputs consolidated without losing original context
  2. Underlying business problem distinguished from requested solution
  3. Ambiguities, contradictions & missing decisions identified early
  4. Contextual clarification questions automatically generated
  5. Analysts & POs review, resolve and approve intended meaning
  6. Approved understanding converted into structured, high-quality specs
  7. Complete end-to-end forward and backward traceability
  8. Change impact assessed across all affected downstream artefacts
  9. Acceptance supported through clear evidence vs informal confirmation
  10. Business outcomes measured against original expected value
FINAL CHALLENGE STATEMENT
"How might we create a reusable, AI-assisted and human-governed business analysis methodology that converts fragmented ideas, conversations, documents, policies and stakeholder viewpoints into challenged, structured, approved and fully traceable requirements—while preserving accountability from the initial business need through implementation, acceptance and measurable value?"
